# 2026-07-15 — Rangefinder-for-barometer altitude fusion (tag `rfnd7`)

Swap the fused altitude source in ORB-SLAM3 from the **barometer** to the **down-facing
rangefinder**, and use a 2 s-smoothed rangefinder altitude as the height GT for evaluation.
No C++ change: `BaroFusion` is altitude-source agnostic (reads `ORB_BARO_CSV` = `(t_ns, alt_m)`),
so we just point it at a rangefinder-derived CSV.

## 1. What the rangefinder actually is (validity)

`dataset/<ds>/ref_rfnd.csv` has header `#timestamp [ns],dist_m` — **distance**, not altitude —
and is already filtered upstream (`nora_bin.rfnd_dist`, `valid_only=True`) to ArduPilot
`RangeFinder::Status::Good == 4`. So the raw dropouts/out-of-range flags are *already gone*; what
remains are Good-status readings whose only residual defects are on-ground clamps and (on 538)
transient noise bursts.

**Surprise vs the brief's hypothesis:** the sensor is **not** short-range. On 538 it reports valid
Good readings continuously up to **138.6 m** and tracks the barometer with `corr = 0.961` — this is
a long-range altimeter (radar-class), not a 40–100 m laser. The "538 is out of range / clamped /
frozen" hypothesis is **false**: there is no ceiling pile-up, no zero-flooding, no frozen runs.

Documented validity criterion (per reading), implemented in `eval/make_rfnd_alt.py`:
1. Good status — guaranteed upstream.
2. Plausible range `dist ∈ [0.30, 200.0] m` — drops on-ground/near-zero clamps; 200 m ceiling is
   above the 138 m max so nothing real is cut.
3. Not a transient spike — `|dist − median_{|t−t_i|≤0.5 s}| ≤ 8 m`.

| flight | Good rows | dropped ground (<0.3 m) | dropped spikes | **valid** | valid range | corr vs baro | residual (rfnd−baro) airborne |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| nora542_half | 2591 | 38 (1.5%) | 0 (0.0%) | **2553 = 98.5%** | 0.30–28.4 m | 0.875 | mean **+2.57**, std **2.30**, [−3.5, +10.5] m |
| nora538_half | 11005 | 49 (0.4%) | 176 (1.6%) | **10780 = 98.0%** | 0.31–138.6 m | 0.961 | mean **+1.46**, std **8.04**, [−34.8, +17.7] m |

Both flights clear the >50% valid-coverage bar, so both are "meaningful" enough to run. **But the
quality differs sharply**, and it is the AGL-vs-altitude physics, not the sensor status, that
decides it:
- **542** flies ~24 m AGL over near-flat ground: rfnd = baro + a nearly-constant **+2.6 m** AGL
  offset, residual std only **2.3 m**. A clean altitude proxy.
- **538** flies ~100–130 m over **relief**: the residual std is **8 m** and dips to **−35 m** (at
  t≈230 s the terrain rises ~30 m under the drone while baro altitude holds ~82 m — AGL shrinks,
  altitude does not). The two quantities genuinely **diverge**. The 1.6% spike bursts (200–300 s)
  are the same relief seen at high horizontal speed.

## 2. Smoothing

`eval/make_rfnd_alt.py` applies the criterion above, then a **2.0 s centered rolling MEAN**
(time-based on the ~20 Hz irregular grid), and writes `dataset/<ds>/ref_rfnd_smooth.csv` in the
exact `ref_baro.csv` format (`#timestamp [ns],alt_m`, integer rebased ns, `%.6f`).

- 542: 2553 valid samples → smoothed range **0.50–27.2 m**.
- 538: 10780 valid samples → smoothed range **0.62–136.8 m**.

## 3. Runs (`rfnd7`)

Binary `ORB_SLAM3/.../mono_inertial_euroc`, config `configs/nora20260709_mi_800_v2.yaml`,
`ORB_TSJUMP_S=6.0 ORB_CLAHE=3.0 ORB_BARO_SCALEREF_S=25`, `ORB_BARO_CSV=…/ref_rfnd_smooth.csv`
(NO `ORB_MAG_CSV`, NO real baro). Paced, `nice -n 10`, one at a time (≤2 mono_inertial machine-wide).
Driver: `eval/run_rfnd7_20260715.sh`. Confirmed `BaroFusion` loaded 2553 (542) / 10780 (538)
samples from the rfnd CSV and fired init scale-edges + periodic scale refinement.

- 542 → `runs/nora542_half/mono_inertial/rfnd7/f_rfnd7.txt`, **1198 poses** (77% of 1561 frames).
- 538 → `runs/nora538_half/mono_inertial/rfnd7/f_rfnd7.txt`, **4126 poses** (98% of 4202 frames).

## 5. Where the AGL-vs-altitude caveat shows in the numbers

1. **As a GT, the rfnd inflates every trajectory's error on 538** — independent of what was fused.
   Take baro6u: vs real_baro it scores 20.21 / 3.27, vs the rfnd_smooth GT it jumps to 25.29 / 9.47.
   The extra ~5 m vATE and the near-**tripled** window-RMS are pure terrain texture that no smooth
   SLAM vertical can (or should) match. On 542 the same swap costs only ~1.5 m vATE (6.90 vs 5.37) —
   flat terrain, small penalty. **The rfnd GT is only as good as the terrain is flat.**
2. **As a fusion source, the rfnd degrades the monocular scale on 538.** rfnd7's recovered
   v-scale collapses to **0.54** vs baro6u's **0.71** (truth 1.0), and it loses on both GTs
   (28.0 vs 20.2 vATE against real baro). Feeding 8 m-std terrain relief into a unary altitude edge
   fights the true climb and pulls the scale down.
3. **On 542 the rfnd is the better fusion source** — beats baro on *both* GTs, including the real
   baro (vATE 4.45 vs 5.37, RMS 2.36 vs 2.52), because at 24 m AGL over flat ground it is a cleaner,
   tighter altitude signal than the drifting barometer, with a near-constant offset the window-datum
   absorbs.

## 6. Verdict

| | as FUSION source | as GT |
|---|---|---|
| **542 (flat, in-range)** | **use rfnd** — beats baro on both GTs (vATE 4.45 vs 5.37) | fine — only ~1.5 m looser than baro; consistent +2.6 m offset |
| **538 (relief, high)** | **keep baro** — rfnd collapses scale to 0.54, loses on both GTs | **do not use rfnd as GT** — terrain texture inflates vATE ~5 m, triples window-RMS; real baro is the honest reference |

Bottom line: the rangefinder is *in range* on both flights (the out-of-range hypothesis was wrong),
but "in range" ≠ "good altitude". AGL equals altitude only over flat terrain; the moment the flight
crosses relief (538), the rangefinder is both a worse fusion input **and** a dishonest GT. The
altitude-source swap is a **win on 542, a regression on 538** — exactly along the flat-vs-relief axis.
